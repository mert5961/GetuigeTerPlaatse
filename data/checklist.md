# First-officer checklist (illustrative, written for this demo)

This is NOT real police procedure. It is a simplified, generic checklist of first-responder principles,
written only to show how a debrief could be grounded in a document. A real version would use the
organisation's own approved training material.

- [C01] Safety first: check whether the suspect may still be present and whether the scene is safe for you and others.
- [C02] Care for the injured: make sure medical help is on its way and ask about the condition of the victim.
- [C03] Check the witness is unhurt and calm enough to talk; reassure her.
- [C04] Identify the witness: full name, address and how to reach her later.
- [C05] Establish the timeline: when things were heard, seen and reported, as exact as possible.
- [C06] Get a description of the suspect: clothing, build, height, gait, face, anything carried.
- [C07] Ask for direction of escape and means of transport.
- [C08] Ask what the witness touched or moved at the scene, to protect evidence and rule out her traces.
- [C09] Ask who else entered the scene, to control contamination and find more witnesses.
- [C10] Ask about digital evidence: cameras, doorbells, phones, devices at or near the scene.
- [C11] Ask about earlier unusual events or people around the building.
- [C12] Check how reliable the observation is: light conditions, distance, eyesight, how long she saw.
- [C13] Use open questions first and avoid suggesting answers (for example "what was he wearing?" instead of "was his jacket green?").
